What to look for around your property

Warm, humid weather can support a variety of household pests. Look for repeated patterns, not a single isolated sign. Some marks have causes other than pests.

Ants and other crawling insects

Watch for repeated trails near food, sinks, plumbing penetrations, windows and baseboards. Note where activity begins and whether it persists despite food being stored securely.

Termites and wood damage

Watch for suspicious mud-like shelter tubes, discarded wings, unusual wood damage or recurring swarmers. These signs are not conclusive; a qualified inspection is the right way to assess suspected termites.

Rodents

Look for fresh droppings, gnaw marks, noises in concealed spaces or damaged food packages. Avoid direct contact with droppings and seek safe cleanup advice before disturbing them.

Mosquitoes and outdoor moisture

Check for standing water in planters, buckets, gutters and outdoor containers. Emptying manageable stagnant-water containers can reduce breeding opportunities. Seek local guidance for persistent problems.

Your 15-minute household observation checklist

  1. Kitchen: Inspect under sinks and around food storage for moisture, residue and recurring pest activity.
  2. Exterior: Note gaps around door sweeps, utility penetrations, vents and window frames. Avoid blocking designed ventilation or making unsafe repairs.
  3. Drainage: Observe standing water near downspouts or exterior storage without entering unsafe areas.
  4. Wood and foundation: Look for obvious changes or unexplained trails and document rather than disturbing suspicious material.
  5. Evidence: Record the approximate date, location and pattern; take your own photos for a qualified professional if needed.
  6. Decision: For persistent activity or suspected structural damage, arrange an independent inspection before committing to treatment.

Common questions

Does one insect mean I have an infestation?

Not necessarily. Isolated observations do not establish the species, source or severity. Document repeated activity and consult a qualified expert when warranted.
